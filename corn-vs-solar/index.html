        How the numbers work

        Corn

        Miles = yield (bu/acre) × ethanol (gal/bu) × ethanol energy ÷ energy a car burns per mile. A car rated on E10 burns 112,114 BTU per gallon of fuel; at 25.5 mpg that is about 4,400 BTU a mile, so a gallon of ethanol (76,330 BTU) drives roughly two-thirds as far as a gallon of E10.

        "Subtract energy used" removes the fossil energy spent on fertilizer, farm diesel, drying, hauling and distilling, using the ratio from USDA's 2015 energy balance: about 2 units of ethanol energy out for each unit of fossil energy in.

        The acre's distillers grains go back to feedlots and dairies, which is why the net land use of ethanol is lower than its gross acreage. That feed value is real but drives no miles, so it is shown and not counted.

        Solar

        Electricity per acre = plant density (MW of panels per fenced acre, from LBNL's survey of 736 U.S. plants; the fixed-tilt default is trimmed from 0.35 to 0.32 so the per-acre gap matches LBNL's measured energy density) × annual yield per kW in that state. Yields come from NLR's PVWatts v8 model at 8 locations, and for the other states from a fit of those results to 10 years of measured irradiance (Open-Meteo ERA5, 2016–2025) at a farming area in each state. The fit is within about 4% of PVWatts at the calibration points.

        From the plant, power loses about 5% on the grid (EIA). Some of it is charged at night, so a share passes through a grid battery (EIA reports about 82% round trip). The EV's EPA rating is measured at the wall and already includes about 12% charging loss; changing the charging efficiency here scales from that.

        "Subtract energy used" removes the energy to build the panels, racking and inverters, spread over the plant's life (an energy payback of about 1.5 years against a 30-year life).

        The corn Sankey counts the grain's chemical energy at about 16.6 MJ per dry kilogram (roughly 99 kWh per bushel), with fermentation losing about 5% of the ethanol's energy as heat; the distillers grains and corn oil carry the rest. Fossil inputs are split about 25% farming, 70% at the mill and 5% hauling, following the shares in USDA's energy balance. The stalks and cobs left in the field are not shown.

        Not counted: water, fertilizer runoff, wildlife, food and feed value, grid upgrades, the energy to build cars and batteries, land value, or the fact that solar farms are often co-located with grazing. This page compares energy and miles only.
