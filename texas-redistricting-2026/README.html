Texas congressional maps: 2026 House vote reconstruction

Open report.html for the maps, results, caveats, and download links.

See FOLLOW_UP.md for the remaining county-data queue, known source issues, and the steps for retrying downloads in coming days and weeks.

This is a November 5, 2026 snapshot, using November 2026 U.S. House votes only. All returns are unofficial. It retabulates party votes geographically; it does not predict which candidates would have run or how voters would have behaved under a different map.

What is ready

  • Official shapefiles for the previous PLANC2193 and the 2025 PLANC2333 plans.
  • Their complete 2020 Census-block equivalency files: 668,757 identical block IDs.
  • Official 2026 House candidate totals for all 254 counties and all 38 districts.
  • Downloaded precinct returns for Bastrop, Bexar, Denton, Harris, Tarrant, Travis, and Williamson. Only reconciled county/district/party cells are used.
  • The latest published statewide precinct geography, 2026 primary, plus the 2024 general vintage for reference.
  • Census block geography and 2020 population/voting-age population for split-unit estimates.
  • Auditable CSVs, vote-conservation checks, and a self-contained HTML report.

Current result

The observed new-map House snapshot contains 9,780,054 votes, with 24 Republican and 14 Democratic district leads.

How precincts enter the calculation

Congressional districts and voting precincts are different geographies. County officials can change precincts independently of redistricting. A precinct may keep its shape and change congressional district; it can also be split by the old map's boundaries.

  1. Join both plans by 2020 Census block ID, never by district number alone.
  2. For each county/new-district reporting cell, identify the possible old districts. If there is only one, all that cell's votes can be assigned without a precinct model.
  3. Where precinct returns are available, require their county/new-district/party sums to equal the state snapshot exactly. Other cells remain at county detail.
  4. Normalize numeric precinct formatting within each county. Do not strip suffixes, match across counties, or assume an old precinct number identifies a current shape.
  5. Intersect a reporting precinct with its actual new district and then the old map. Single-old-district pieces are assigned directly.
  6. For split pieces, allocate each party's actual votes in proportion to Census voting-age population. Inhabited blocks are assigned to primary precincts by an interior point; official block files supply their old/new congressional districts. This treats the party vote as uniform within the reporting unit after population weighting. Total population and land area provide sensitivity scenarios.
  7. Keep missing county detail unresolved. A district with missing major-party county detail remains pending even when the votes already located suggest a leader.

The model allocates 6,236,936 votes to old districts and retains 3,543,118 votes without an allocation. Those figures sum to the actual statewide House count in every scenario. Approximately 2,371 split-unit votes use an area fallback because the population crosswalk provides no applicable weight.

Checks and limits

  • Every state's-feed House candidate total equals the sum of its county rows.
  • All 38 district major-party totals agree with the state's separate HTML summary.
  • Each parsed precinct source is checked against its own choice/method totals.
  • Each county/district/party cell must match the state snapshot to be used at precinct detail. Bexar's major-party totals do not yet match and stay unresolved. Minor-party and write-in label/snapshot differences in other counties are also retained as uncertainty.
  • 412 votes have unmatched precinct labels, including F99 suffixes. No geographic interpretation is invented for them.
  • 79 accepted votes conflict with the primary precinct/new-district geometry and are left unresolved.
  • All positive old-map polygon overlaps are retained for conservative bounds, even tiny slivers. Bounds may therefore be much wider than plausible errors. They are not statistical intervals and district-specific maxima cannot be summed.
  • The primary precinct map still needs confirmation against November boundaries.
  • Census weights represent 2020 residents, not 2026 voters. They cannot measure within-precinct party differences, new development, or turnout changes.
  • No 2024 election vote is used in the 2026 reconstruction. The legislature's downloaded 2024 two-plan comparison is reference material only.

Reproduce

From this directory:

python3 -m venv .venv
.venv/bin/pip install -r requirements.txt
.venv/bin/python scripts/fetch_sources.py --county-indexes
.venv/bin/python scripts/analyze.py
.venv/bin/python scripts/estimate.py
.venv/bin/python scripts/build_report.py

Existing sources are cached. fetch_sources.py --refresh --county-indexes re-downloads mutable sources and can change the snapshot. County vendor indexes are incomplete; failed/index-missing downloads are recorded, not interpreted as absence of results. Some published county reports may require additional collection and parsers.

The population-weight cache is keyed to source hashes and the required precincts. The large Census block shapefile is streamed rather than loaded into memory as one map.

The optional --model-missing-counties estimator mode is not used in this report; it would impose a separate and much weaker within-county population-allocation model.
