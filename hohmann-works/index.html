Orbital logistics

Hohmann Works

Build a factory around a planet where nothing sits still and nothing travels in a straight line.

  • Lower orbits are faster. Stations drift past each other all the time.
  • Cargo flies half an ellipse. A route launches a pod on a Hohmann transfer. It arrives on the far side of the planet from where it left, a fixed number of seconds later.
  • Routes wait for a launch window. A pod only launches when the receiver sits at the right lead angle, marked by the amber bracket. Far-apart orbits get windows often. Close orbits wait a long time.
  • Every launch burns fuel. Bigger orbit changes cost more Δv, paid in credits. A pod that misses its catch keeps coasting on its ellipse and gets a few more chances.
