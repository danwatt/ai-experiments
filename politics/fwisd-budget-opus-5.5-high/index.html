School meals and the SNAP connection

Every Fort Worth ISD student eats free under the federal Community Eligibility Provision (CEP). How much USDA pays the district for those meals depends on the share of students who are directly certified: matched to SNAP, TANF, foster care, homeless or migrant records instead of filing a paper application. Fewer SNAP households means fewer directly certified students, and that lowers the reimbursement for the same meal.

From the district's food-service fund in TEA PEIMS (enterprise fund 701; special revenue fund 240 from FY2026). Commodities are USDA Foods valued at cost. Pandemic-era summer and year-round meals (Seamless Summer Option) are booked as breakfast and lunch.

What to watch

  • The CEP rate for a school year is set from students identified by April 1 of the year before. The 2025-26 drop was locked in before H.R. 1 (signed July 4, 2025) changed SNAP rules, so the law didn't cause it.
  • H.R. 1 widens SNAP work requirements and moves part of SNAP's cost to states starting in federal FY2028. Fewer SNAP households would mean fewer directly certified students and a lower CEP claiming rate in later years.
  • If the free share drops further, the district either covers more of the meal cost from local and state money or reconsiders district-wide CEP. The district says all students eat free in 2026-27.
  • After-school suppers (CACFP at-risk) are new since fall 2024 and bring in about $1.8 million a year.

Sources and method

  • Actuals FY2011–FY2025: Texas Education Agency, PEIMS district actual financial data (audited, every fund/function/object), filtered to district 220905. "All funds" means the General Fund, food service, federal and state/local grant funds, Debt Service and Capital Projects. Internal-service and fiduciary funds are left out so money isn't counted twice. Bond proceeds, refundings and transfers between funds show up only as the balancing node "Fund balance, bonds & transfers".
  • Adopted budgets FY2023–FY2027: Fort Worth ISD budget documents: Budget Books 2022-23, 2023-24 and 2024-25 (All Funds table), and the 2024-25 and 2026-27 Adopted Budget postings. The posted 2025-26 adopted budget covers only the General Fund, so that year's Food Service and Debt Service figures come from the district's PEIMS budget submission (TEA budget2026). The 2026-27 posting has no fund split. Budgets cover only the three funds Texas requires a board to adopt, so most federal grants (Title I, IDEA, ESSER) aren't in them. That's why the actuals are the better source for federal trends.
  • Federal program categories follow TEA fund codes: 211/276 Title I; 224–226 IDEA-B; 266 (FY2020+), 281, 282, 284, 285, 279 COVID relief; 266 (FY2011), 283–287 (FY2011–13) the 2009 stimulus and Education Jobs; General Fund objects 5931/5932 Medicaid (SHARS, administrative claiming); food-service fund objects 7952–7955 / 5921–5923 USDA child nutrition.
  • School meals: Texas Department of Agriculture open data on data.texas.gov: School Nutrition Programs meal reimbursement (program years 2015-16 to 2025-26), Seamless Summer Option and CACFP at-risk reimbursement, contracting entity 01020.
  • Enrollment: TEA Summarized PEIMS Actual Financial Data, fall survey enrollment. For FY2026 and FY2027 budgets, per-student values use the enrollment implied by the district's own per-pupil figures in the 2026-27 budget summary.
  • Inflation: CPI-U (not seasonally adjusted) from FRED, averaged July–June to match the district fiscal year. FY2027 uses July–September 2026 only.
  • Fiscal years run July 1 to June 30 and are named for the year they end, so FY2025 is the 2024-25 school year. Rebuild with build/fetch.sh and build/build_data.py.
