How timing and practice selection work

A round has 10 unique facts. Up to six are drawn from previously practiced facts, with extra weight for misses, hints, and slower responses; the rest introduce new facts when available. Addition uses addends from 0 to 10 and the selected total limit. Subtraction uses the same fact families, so answers stay nonnegative and at most 10.

First-response time measures active thinking until the first answer, hint, or reveal. Active round time adds the time spent solving each fact, including retries. It excludes feedback, pauses, and time while the tab is hidden. First-try accuracy means a correct first answer without help. Round averages include revealed and helped facts; this is practice data, not an assessment. Recent rounds include partial rounds. The latest 200 rounds are retained; fact totals and bloom totals continue accumulating.
