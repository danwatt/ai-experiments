How Texas grew a duck, and how batteries are flattening it

A step-by-step walk through the average day on the ERCOT grid, built from ERCOT's 15-minute fuel mix data. Let it play, or step through at your own pace.

Reading the chart

  • Every line is an average day: each 15-minute slot is averaged over every day of the season (spring is March to May, summer June to August). Times are Central, and each value is for the 15 minutes ending at that time.
  • Demand is all generation settled on the ERCOT grid, minus power used to charge batteries. Net load is demand minus wind, solar and net battery output: what gas, coal, nuclear and the rest have to supply.
  • The evening ramp is the steepest three-hour rise in net load that starts between noon and 9 pm.
  • Batteries are ERCOT's "Other" category (mostly battery discharge) plus WSL (battery charging). Rooftop solar is not in ERCOT's market data, so it shows up as lower demand rather than as solar.
  • The optional voiceover reads the same numbers as the captions. It was generated with a local Voicebox server (the Heart voice) by build_voiceover.py.
  • Source: ERCOT Fuel Mix Report. For every season and month, see the time-of-day charts in the explorer.
