What the animation computes

Each one follows the plan Ramsey's Ben and Arthur chart gives Arthur: $2,000 into the S&P 500 at the start of every year from age 27 through 65, 39 deposits. At 12% a year that reaches the chart's $1,532,166 (this model gives $1,532,183; the chart rounds each year). After the second chapter you choose how to count: plain dollars (a flat $2,000 a year and nominal balances, as in Ramsey's chart) or today's dollars (each deposit buys what $2,000 bought in November 2026, the latest month of Shiller's price data, and balances are shown at November 2026 prices). Returns are calendar-year total returns with dividends: Shiller's data for 1872–1927, Damodaran's from 1928. Ben, the father, starts in one year and his son Arthur 25 years later, so they share 14 years. The explainer uses the 43 thirty-nine-year windows that start from 1945 on (1945–1983 through 1987–2025), which give 18 father/son pairs.

Caveats

  • A historical simulation, not a forecast or financial advice.
  • No fees or taxes; dividends reinvested.
  • Ramsey's own Ben and Arthur chart is a different setup (Ben invests early and stops). Here both father and son follow Arthur's plan, age 27 through 65, and only the years differ.
