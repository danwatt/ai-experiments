    What the animation computes

    Each retiree starts with $1,000,000 in the S&P 500 (dividends reinvested, no fees or taxes) on January 1 and withdraws 8% of that starting amount at the end of every year for 30 years. Returns are calendar-year total returns: Shiller's data before 1928, Damodaran's from 1928. Retirements start every year from 1945 to 2025; the 52 that began by 1996 have a full 30 years of history, and later ones are shown as far as the data goes.

    After the second chapter you choose how to count. Plain dollars: the same $80,000 check every year, never raised, with balances as the statement showed them. Today's dollars: $80,000 of buying power every year (the check rises with inflation), with balances shown at November 2026 prices, the latest month of Shiller's price data.

    Caveats

    • A historical simulation, not a forecast or financial advice.
    • Ramsey's world (chapter 2) uses their own numbers: 12% returns, 4% inflation, an $80,000 first withdrawal raised 4% a year.
    • Ramsey's site quotes the 8% framework in terms of growth stock mutual funds averaging 10–12%; this page uses the S&P 500 itself, with no fund fees.
