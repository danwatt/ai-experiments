What the animation computes

The same models as the “What if I'm half wrong?” page, from the same data. Inflation is the December to December change in consumer prices, averaged by compounding. Returns are calendar-year total returns: the S&P 500 index itself (dividends reinvested, no costs) back to 1926, and Ramsey's four American Funds (AIVSX, AGTHX, CWGIX, ANWPX), 25% each, from 1981, with New Perspective standing in for Capital World Growth and Income before 1994. The “simple average” adds up the yearly returns and divides; the “compounded” rate is the steady yearly rate that gets the money from start to finish.

Saving: $625 at the start of every month for 40 years, each year's return spread evenly over its months; “in the money of the year they started” divides by the rise in prices over the 40 years. The perfect world: exactly 12% a year (0.949% a month) and 4% inflation, $40,000 from $500,000 raised 4% each January, paid monthly or all on January 1. Real history: $500,000, $40,000 the first year in twelve monthly payments, raised each January by the past year's actual inflation; a retirement keeps up if at the end it still buys what $500,000 did at the start. Retirements still underway are judged as they stood at the end of 2025.

The data and the quotes

  • Prices and S&P 500 returns are the series the simulator uses (../data.js): Robert Shiller's data to 1927 and Aswath Damodaran's from 1928. Fund returns are explainer 4's (../explainer-4/funds.js): calendar-year returns at NAV, after yearly fund costs but not the Class A sales charge.
  • The quotes are from chapter 9 of The Total Money Makeover (first published September 2003); on screen they are shortened, with the cuts marked.
  • The book's $7,588,545 is close to what 1% a month makes ($7.43 million with deposits at the start of each month); a steady 12% a year makes $6.12 million.
  • Roth IRAs began in 1998, and $7,500 a year is more than early IRA limits allowed; like the book, this ignores both, and taxes.
  • A historical simulation, not a forecast or financial advice.
