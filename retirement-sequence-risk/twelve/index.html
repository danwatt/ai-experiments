Did stocks make 12%? Every 5, 10, 20 and 30-year window

In The Total Money Makeover, Dave Ramsey says you should make 12 percent on your money over time in good growth stock mutual funds, pointing to the historical averages of the S&P 500. Each square below is one window of years, ending in the year under it: blue where the funds made the target, 12% a year or more, red where they fell short. Ramsey's figure is 12%; the slider moves the target from 10% to 13%. Switch between the simple average of the years, the figure Ramsey quotes, and the compound rate the money actually grew at.

Yearly fund costs are already in these returns. Each fund pays its expenses out of its own assets, so every fund figure on this page is after them: 0.55% to 0.83% a year for the American Funds' Class A shares (about 0.24 of that a 12b-1 fee that pays the broker who sold them) and 0.14% to 0.18% for VFINX, per their prospectuses and annual reports from 1998 to 2025. Left out is the sales charge of up to 5.75% paid when buying Class A shares, except where the lifetime hover shows it. The S&P 500 index rows further down carry no costs at all.

Notes

  • Simple average adds up each year's return and divides by the number of years. Compound (CAGR) is the steady yearly rate that turns the starting money into the ending money. Ups and downs pull the compound rate below the average: +50% then −50% averages 0%, but loses a quarter of the money.
  • All returns are nominal, before inflation, as Ramsey states the 12%. Windows overlap, so neighboring squares share most of their years and are not independent tries.
  • Fund returns are calendar-year total returns at NAV, with distributions reinvested and fund expenses already taken out, but not the sales charge paid when buying Class A shares: the same published figures as the four-funds explainer, where the data table links every source. Capital World Growth and Income (CWGIX) began in 1993, so New Perspective (ANWPX) stands in for 1981–1993. The S&P 500 index fund is Vanguard 500 Index (VFINX).
  • Lifetime returns run from the day each fund began (for Growth Fund of America, the day Capital Research began managing it) and are at net asset value, without the sales charge. Vanguard 500 Index began in August 1976; its lifetime here starts at the end of 1976, as Vanguard's own 25-year figure does.
  • "Four funds, 25% each" averages the four funds' returns each year, as if put back to a quarter each every January.
  • The S&P 500 since 1872 is from Robert Shiller to 1927 and Aswath Damodaran (NYU Stern) from 1928, the series the simulator uses.
